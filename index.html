Where Empires Rose & Enlightenment Dawned

"Few regions on Earth have shaped human thought, ethics, and scholarship as deeply as the ancient soil of Bihar."

Derived from the Sanskrit word Vihara, meaning Buddhist monasteries, Bihar was the epicenter of Magadhan, Mauryan, and Gupta dynasties. Here at Pataliputra, Emperor Ashoka governed an empire spanning from the Hindu Kush to Bengal, later dedicating his reign to universal peace and dhamma.

Today, Bihar invites discerning travelers on a journey through tranquil monastic gardens, ancient archaeological wonders, mist-covered Sal forests in the Himalayan foothills, and living cultural traditions that continue to flourish with grace.

Academic Legacy

Home to Nalanda and Vikramshila, the world’s earliest residential universities.

Bodh Gaya

UNESCO Site · Ancient University

Nalanda

The ancient world’s premier residential university that flourished for seven centuries, attracting scholars across Asia with vast libraries, stupas, and excavated monastic cells.

Ancient Capital · Hill Sanctuary

Rajgir

Nestled in a green valley among seven hills, Rajgir features the gleaming white Vishwa Shanti Stupa, aerial ropeway chairlift, Griddhakuta Vulture’s Peak, and rejuvenating hot springs.

Capital City · Ancient Pataliputra

Patna

The historic capital along the holy Ganges river. Marvel at the beehive Golghar, the world-class Bihar Museum, Mauryan Kumhrar ruins, and the sacred Takht Sri Patna Sahib.

Historic Republic · Cradle of Democracy

Vaishali

Birthplace of Lord Mahavira and capital of the world’s first republic. Marvel at Emperor Ashoka’s polished monolithic Lion Pillar, the Buddha Relic Stupa, and sacred coronation tanks.

Immersive Experiences

Living Traditions & Gastronomy

Beyond stone and monuments, Bihar’s soul thrives in its vibrant festivals, age-old crafts, and hearty culinary treasures.

Witness the devotion of Chhath Puja as thousands greet the dawn on the sacred Ganges ghats. Taste authentic Litti Chokha drenched in golden ghee, savory Champaran meat cooked in earthen handis, and admire GI-tagged Madhubani paintings made by master artisans.
